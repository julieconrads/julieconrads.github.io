## Overview

The Quagga mussel (_Dreissena rostriformis bugensis_) has displaced the closely related Zebra mussel (_Dreissena polymorpha_) in many invaded systems. One key to its success may lie in its reproductive biology. This project examines the reproductive output of Quagga mussels in Lake Constance — the first Swiss lake to be invaded (2014) — across different depths and seasons.

---

## Key Questions

- Does reproductive success vary with depth? The Quagga mussel's tolerance for deep, soft sediments distinguishes it from the Zebra mussel — does this extend to reproductive performance?
- Are there seasonal patterns in gonad development and spawning?
- What does reproductive variation across depths and seasons tell us about why this species is outcompeting the Zebra mussel?

---

## Significance

Understanding what makes the Quagga mussel so ecologically successful is essential for predicting its spread and impact in newly invaded systems like Lake Zurich. Reproductive advantage — whether in fecundity, timing, or depth range — could explain why established Quagga populations so consistently dominate over Zebra mussels.

A manuscript is in preparation.
